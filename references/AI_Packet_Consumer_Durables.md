# AI Stock Review Packet: Consumer Durables

**Version 2.2.5 — Last updated 2026-10-08.** Supersedes v2.2.4. v2.2.5 routes pre-revenue vehicle and eVTOL developers to the Pre-Revenue Overlay. v2.2.4 supersedes v2.1 (archived as `AI_Packet_Consumer_Durables_v2_1.md`). Upgrades the packet from a general sector guide to a strict execution file: v4 verification and provenance standard, the four shared rules (incl. the expectations module at the v2.2.4 strict-packet level), routing to six branches (auto OEMs, auto suppliers, homebuilders, household durables, leisure products, apparel/footwear/luxury brands), each with its own metrics, red flags and pillar definitions; cycle-position rules (macro cycle for A–E, brand cycle for F); inventory and channel rules with a currency-basis test; captive-finance separation; scoring, audit-trail and five-category conclusion rules. Branches A–E keep the v2.1 pillar weights (25/25/20/15/15) so scores stay comparable with earlier reviews; only branch F reweights. Educational framework — not investment advice. No buy/sell/hold language.

*Automobiles and auto components, homebuilders, household durables (appliances, furniture, housewares, consumer electronics), leisure products (RVs, boats, powersports, toys), and textiles, apparel, footwear and luxury goods* — GICS: Consumer Discretionary (Automobiles & Components; Consumer Durables & Apparel).

**Core principle: route to the branch, then establish cycle position, before rating anything.** A 65%-gross-margin footwear brand and a 15%-gross-margin RV maker sit in the same GICS group but share almost no economics. Within each branch, the classic trap is a low P/E on peak-cycle earnings — durables look cheapest right before demand turns. Verified does not mean Positive.

## Prompt

> Analyze [company name / ticker] using the uploaded Core Framework and this Consumer Durables guide.
> Do not use buy/sell/hold language.
> Provide: 1. Business model summary · 2. Sector classification and branch (A–F) · 3. Peer group with rationale · 4. Key branch metrics with provenance labels · 5. Values vs peers and 5-year history · 6. Positive/Watch/Negative assessment with stated cycle position · 7. Severe red flags, if any · 8. Valuation (mid-cycle basis) · 9. Data confidence: High/Medium/Low · 10. Neutral research conclusion: strong on current evidence / mixed, needs more evidence / weak on current evidence / high-risk, special situation / insufficient data.
> Use primary sources (10-K, 10-Q, 20-F, 6-K, annual reports, earnings releases, delivery/order releases). Do not rely only on screeners or summaries.

## Universal checklist

1. Identify the business model and branch. For mixed businesses, classify by where most normalized operating profit comes from.
2. Revenue trend over 5 years — volume vs price/mix, organic vs acquired, constant-currency vs reported.
3. Gross and operating margin vs own 5–10 year range and vs peers **in the same branch**.
4. Inventory and channel health — own inventory, dealer/retailer inventory, shipments vs end demand.
5. Free cash flow — positive, converting from profits? Industrial FCF only where a captive finance arm exists.
6. Leverage — net debt/EBITDA (industrial basis, lease-adjusted where leases are material); debt-to-capital for homebuilders.
7. Share count over 5 years — period-end, split-adjusted.
8. Returns — ROIC through the cycle; ROE with P/B for homebuilders.
9. Valuation vs own 5-year range AND vs peers, on a mid-cycle basis.
10. Run the branch red-flag list; grade findings Minor / Moderate / Severe.
11. Ask why the opportunity exists; write the thesis in two sentences.

**Peer group:** 5–10 names sharing branch, business model, geography, price tier, channel mix, capital intensity, and cyclicality. A sector label is not a peer group.

## Branch routing map

Classify by where most **normalized operating profit** comes from, not revenue. Then use only that branch's section plus the shared rules.

| Branch | Use when most profit comes from | First-pass metrics |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Auto OEMs** | Designing, building and selling light vehicles, trucks, motorcycles sold through dealers or direct (incl. EV makers) | Deliveries & share, net pricing/incentives, dealer days' supply, automotive EBIT margin (ex captive finance), industrial FCF & net cash |
| **B. Auto suppliers** | Components, systems and tires sold to OEMs and the aftermarket | Growth over market (content per vehicle), EBIT margin, customer concentration, booked business, net leverage |
| **C. Homebuilders** | Building and selling homes on owned or optioned land | Net orders, cancellation rate, backlog, homebuilding gross margin, incentives, land/lot position, net debt-to-capital, ROE vs P/B |
| **D. Household durables** | Appliances, furniture, housewares, tools, branded consumer electronics | Organic growth (volume vs price), gross margin vs own range, retailer channel inventory, housing-turnover sensitivity, net debt/EBITDA |
| **E. Leisure products** | RVs, boats, powersports, golf/fitness equipment, toys and games | Wholesale vs retail sales, dealer/retailer inventory, gross margin vs own range, seasonality, net debt/EBITDA |
| **F. Apparel, footwear & luxury** | Brand owners that design, source and sell apparel, footwear, accessories, leather goods, watches and jewelry through wholesale and/or DTC | Constant-currency growth, DTC/wholesale mix, gross margin & markdowns, inventory-to-sales spread, brand concentration, cash conversion |

**Route out:** auto dealers, auto-parts retailers, multi-brand apparel/footwear retailers and home-furnishing retailers → **Retail**. Building products sold to contractors (windows, plumbing, HVAC) → **Producer Manufacturing**. Household consumables (detergent, paper goods, cosmetics sold mainly through grocery/mass channels) → **Consumer Staples**; prestige beauty owned by a luxury group stays with that group's dominant segment. Hotels, timeshare and cruise → **Consumer Services**. Pre-revenue vehicle or eVTOL developers whose core commercial revenue is immaterial → **Pre-Revenue Overlay** (core-revenue materiality test runs before this packet's routing).

**Routing notes:** a mono-brand company that designs its own products but sells mostly through its own stores (vertical brand) stays in **F** — brand economics dominate — and borrows Retail's comps and store-productivity metrics as an overlay. A two- or three-wheeler maker is **A**. A captive finance arm never decides the branch — see Mixed businesses & captive finance.

## Consumer Durables metric verification standard

A branch metric is not "verified" unless the output includes all seven evidence items:

1. Metric name
2. Exact value
3. Source document
4. Exact table, note, or section
5. Short quote showing the metric label next to the value
6. Definition match to this guide (or the company definition, stated)
7. Score impact

Rules: search summaries, aggregator snippets, and unlabeled extracted numbers cannot verify branch metrics. Never reuse a percentage from a nearby table unless the label exactly matches the intended metric. If the label is not visible next to the value, the metric is not verified. If the metric cannot be verified, mark it "Needs verification."

**Known traps:** "deliveries" vs "wholesales" vs "retail sales" vs "registrations" are four different volume measures. "Backlog" for homebuilders is signed contracts (units and value); for suppliers "booked business" is nonbinding lifetime-revenue awards — never treat the two alike. Homebuilding gross margin may include or exclude capitalized interest and impairments — state which. Constant-currency growth is company-defined and non-GAAP/non-IFRS. Automotive margins must exclude captive finance; consolidated net debt includes captive-finance debt that funds receivables, not operations. Gross margin for brands may include or exclude distribution, freight and duty costs — check the cost-of-sales policy before comparing peers.

## Provenance labels (v4 standard)

Every key metric carries exactly one label. The label reflects what the review itself verified, not how plausible the number is:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | The review itself directly read the source document and quotes the exact label next to the value |
| **Calculated — verified inputs** | Derived by a stated, reproducible formula whose inputs are each individually Verified. Derived ratios (FCF margin, coverage ratios, EV multiples) are never plain "Verified" |
| **User-provided filing excerpt** | The user pasted verbatim filing text containing the label and value; usable for rating decisions with provenance stated, but not "Verified" |
| **Reported, pending direct verification** | The value came from another AI, an evaluator, a search summary, a secondary source, or a relayed table |
| **Estimated** | Derived from market data, aggregators, or calculation with unverified or mixed inputs; "Estimated proxy" is its sub-type |
| **Needs verification** | An important metric not found or not confirmed |
| **Not disclosed** | The relevant primary sources were checked and do not contain the metric — a disclosure fact, not a performance judgment |

Rules: external confirmation never upgrades a label to Verified — only directly reading the source does. "Reported" is Medium confidence at most and never enough by itself to upgrade a pillar. A score can use reported or estimated data cautiously; the label tells the reader how much to trust it. A derived ratio takes "Calculated — verified inputs" only when every input meets the verification standard; if any input is weaker, the ratio takes the weakest input's label. Only Verified or Calculated — verified inputs evidence can activate a Severe-flag score cap.

## Shared rules

**Conflicting figures rule (universal):** when two figures for the same metric appear to conflict, do not choose one immediately. First classify the difference: 1. different scope — group vs segment, consolidated vs ex-subsidiary · 2. different basis — gross vs net, reported vs adjusted · 3. different period — quarterly, annualized, LTM, fiscal year · 4. different currency · 5. different definition — company-defined vs packet-defined. Show both figures with provenance labels, then rate the pillar based on the figure that best matches the packet definition. If neither figure matches the packet definition, keep the pillar at Watch or Needs verification.

**Estimated proxy:** when a metric is calculated from available inputs but does not match the company's exact disclosed definition, label it **"Estimated proxy"** and state the inputs used. Examples: an AFFO payout proxy estimated above 100% when AFFO itself is not disclosed; an interest coverage proxy estimated from verified income-statement inputs but not company-disclosed; a net debt/EBITDAre proxy estimated when EBITDAre is not disclosed or JV/pro-rata treatment is unresolved. Never imply the proxy is the same as the company's official metric: a proxy is a sub-type of Estimated, is never Verified, never High confidence, and any rating that rests on it must say so.

**Guidance is not achievement:** guidance, targets, and management plans can support a Watch or directional comment, but they should not receive full Positive credit unless supported by achieved results, binding regulatory approval, or directly verified realized performance. This applies especially to: utilities rate-base growth guidance · REIT rent ramp / stabilized rent targets · energy production or capex targets · SaaS margin expansion targets · bank medium-term ROE targets.

**Expectations are not results (universal):** a price already contains a forecast, and a review that only compares multiples never states what that forecast is. Run this test whenever the valuation pillar would otherwise rest on a multiple above the company's own 5-year range, or whenever the market or management thesis rests on segments with no achieved results; otherwise record "expectations test not triggered" and move on. Invert the valuation — hold the current price fixed and solve for what it requires: revenue CAGR over a stated horizon, steady-state operating or FCF margin, reinvestment (sales-to-capital or reinvestment rate), the discount rate with its components, and a terminal growth rate no higher than the long-run risk-free rate. Convert the requirement into absolute end-of-horizon revenue and test it three ways: against the company's own achieved 5-year record, against the best verified peer analog at comparable scale, and against the current size of the market being addressed. **TAM × assumed share is not a valuation output — it is the assumption under test.** Where a sector packet defines an expectations adaptation, the adaptation governs the variables solved for and the value split; the triggers, caps and labels of this module still apply.

**Proven vs unproven enterprise value:** split EV into the part the proven business supports under the sector packet's normal valuation method and the residual the market is paying for optionality. A segment counts as proven only when the company discloses its revenue AND either its operating profit or a company-defined unit economic; anything else is unproven, is valued separately and labeled, and is never blended into the base multiple. Report the residual as a share of total EV. Rules: required inputs that exceed anything the company has achieved — or anything a verified peer has achieved at comparable scale — cap the valuation pillar at Watch unless the review names the specific verified evidence supporting the higher requirement · residual EV above ~50% of total EV caps the valuation pillar at Watch and obliges the conclusion to state the residual share, whatever the operating pillars show · disclosure too thin to build the test (no segment detail, no margin path) makes valuation **Needs verification**, never a favorable default · do not penalize the same optimism twice by both discounting the required growth and inflating the discount rate — pick one and say which.

**Estimated — expectations-implied:** every output of this test carries that label, a sub-type of Estimated. It is never Verified and never High confidence, because the solution depends on assumptions the review itself chose; only the inputs (price, period-end share count, net debt, disclosed segment figures) carry labels of their own. **Avoid:** analyst price targets as evidence of value · forward multiples built on margins the company has never achieved · "cheap versus its own history" when that history sits entirely inside one expansion regime · peer multiples where the peers are themselves priced on expectations.

In this packet "guidance is not achievement" also covers: supplier booked business · homebuilder community-count and closing guidance · EV delivery and cost-down targets · brand mid-term targets (growth, margin, store openings) · announced tariff mitigation or tariff refunds.

## Quality of earnings

Profits converting into cash? Over 3–5 years FCF should roughly track net income. FCF consistently and materially below net income? Investigate the gap. 'Adjusted' earnings much higher than GAAP, year after year? Same 'one-time' add-backs recurring every year? Working capital temporarily flattering cash flow? Acquisitions masking weak organic growth? Watch goodwill build. Revenue recognized aggressively? Receivables outgrowing revenue is a warning. Margin gains from real operating leverage — or accounting changes? In this sector also check: shipments pushed into dealers or wholesale accounts ahead of end demand (channel stuffing shows up as receivables and channel inventory outgrowing sales) · warranty and recall reserves falling as a share of revenue while claims rise · regulatory-credit or emissions-credit sales inflating automotive profit · homebuilder land and inventory impairments recurring every downturn · SBC excluded from "adjusted EBITDA" (read SBC from the cash-flow statement and state the gap) · FX gains/losses moving net income for non-USD reporters.
Rule: high-quality earnings are recurring, cash-backed, not dependent on repeated adjustments.

## Geography/currency

Where is revenue generated? Where are profits generated (often different)? Where are assets located? Can they be taken or stranded? What currencies are revenue and costs in? Any mismatch? What currency is the debt in? FX debt vs local revenue is the classic blow-up. Politically risky jurisdictions — expropriation, licenses, rule of law? Could tariffs, sanctions, capital controls, tax changes, or regulation hit the model? In this sector also state: **sourcing concentration** (share of production from one country; forced-labor import rules such as UFLPA detentions) · **tariff exposure and pass-through** (absorbed in margin vs priced through; refunds are guidance, not achievement, until received) · **translation gap** — for non-USD reporters show reported and constant-currency growth side by side; never rate growth on one without stating the other.

## Cycle-position rules

Every review must state an explicit cycle position with the evidence behind it.

**Branches A–E (macro durables cycle):** **early upturn / mid-cycle / late cycle-peak / downturn / undetermined.** Evidence, in order of usefulness: own and channel inventory trend · orders, backlog and cancellations · pricing and incentives · margin vs own 5–10 year range · industry volume data (SAAR, housing starts, RV/boat retail registrations — Estimated at best) · rate and affordability context.

**Branch F (brand cycle — demand is brand-specific, not macro):** **accelerating / steady growth / decelerating / reset (declining or rebasing) / undetermined.** Evidence: constant-currency growth trend over 4–8 quarters · DTC vs wholesale divergence · inventory-to-sales spread · gross margin and markdown commentary · wholesale order book or "futures" where disclosed · marketing intensity needed to hold growth. A brand can be in reset during a macro boom, and vice versa.

Rules: margins at or near a 5–10 year high are peak evidence, not quality evidence — do not extrapolate them. Margin-vs-own-range informs the STATED cycle phase but earns no points in the cycle pillar (it already scores in the margins pillar). A low trailing P/E combined with peak-range margins rates the valuation pillar Watch at best, never Positive on cheapness alone. A high trailing P/E at trough margins is not automatically Negative. If cycle position cannot be estimated from evidence, state "cycle position: undetermined", cap BOTH the cycle/channel pillar and the valuation pillar at Watch, and cap the overall conclusion at "mixed, needs more evidence" regardless of the numerical score. Rate growth on the multi-year trend; annualizing a single peak or trough quarter is never acceptable.

## Inventory & channel rules

**Own inventory:** compute inventory days (DIO) on the COGS basis and rate the trend vs own history, not a universal threshold. **Inventory-to-sales spread** = YoY inventory growth − YoY revenue growth, same reporting currency, same quarter-end. **Currency-basis test:** inventory is translated at period-end rates and revenue at average rates — when the reporting currency moved more than ~5% against the main sales currencies, show the spread on reported figures, state the direction of the translation effect, and apply the conflicting figures rule before rating.

Bands (all branches): spread ≤ +5 pp → Positive · +5 to +15 pp, or above +15 pp for a single quarter → Watch · above +15 pp for two or more consecutive quarters → Negative and a Moderate flag. **Severe** only when a verified inventory build combines with verified discounting (gross margin decline attributed to markdowns, incentives, or promotions; inventory write-downs; liquidation into off-price or fleet channels) AND falling revenue or cut guidance. A strategic build claimed by management (product launch, supply security, tariff pre-buy) without sell-through evidence stays Watch — guidance is not achievement.

**Channel inventory:** dealer days' supply (A), retailer weeks on hand (D, E, F), dealer inventory units (E). Shipments (wholesale) growing faster than end demand (retail sales, registrations, sell-through) for two or more quarters is channel fill — a Moderate flag. If channel data is **Not disclosed**, rely on own inventory, receivables vs revenue, and management commentary — and say so; do not silently substitute an aggregator's estimate.

## A. Auto OEMs

Capital-intensive, cyclical, high fixed cost, thin margins at mass-market scale; premium and luxury makers earn structurally more. Profit swings with volume, pricing/incentives, mix, and regulation (emissions rules, EV mandates, credits, tariffs). Separate the automotive business from any captive finance arm before rating anything.

| Metric | Positive | Watch | Negative | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deliveries / wholesales & share** | Growing, share stable or rising | Flat or share slipping | Falling, share loss | State which volume measure; share vs the company's own markets |
| **Net pricing & incentives** | Price/mix positive, incentives below segment average | Incentives rising | Incentives rising with falling ASP | Incentives as % of transaction price where disclosed |
| **Dealer days' supply** | < 60 days | 60–90 days | > 90 days and rising | US convention; use company or industry definition, stated; Not disclosed for direct-sales EV makers → use own inventory |
| **Automotive EBIT margin** (ex captive finance) | > 8% | 4–8% | < 4% | Mass-market band; judge premium/luxury OEMs vs own peer set |
| **Industrial / automotive FCF** | Positive, covers dividends | Positive, thin | Negative | Excludes captive-finance receivables growth |
| **Automotive net cash / net debt** | Net cash or < 1x EBITDA | 1–2x | > 2x | Exclude captive-finance debt; include pension deficits where material |
| **Warranty & recall cost** | Stable % of revenue | Rising | Rising sharply or under-reserved | Read the warranty reserve roll-forward |
| **Regulatory-credit dependence** | < 5% of operating income | 5–20% | > 20% | Credit revenue is policy-dependent and can vanish |

**EV and early-stage overlay:** (companies with immaterial core revenue go to the Pre-Revenue Overlay instead) for revenue-generating makers with negative or near-zero automotive gross margin, add cash runway (trailing AND forward, funding the next production milestone), gross profit per vehicle, cash burn, and period-end dilution; rate the balance-sheet pillar on runway, not leverage. Non-auto segments (energy storage, autonomy, robotics, software) are proven only with disclosed revenue and segment profit — otherwise value them as unproven under the expectations module.

**What NOT to use:** consolidated net debt including captive finance · low P/E at peak volumes and pricing · deliveries without pricing/incentive context · EV unit growth without unit economics · announced cost-down or launch timelines as achievement.

**Red flags:** **Severe** — dealer inventory pile-up forcing heavy incentives with falling ASP (verified) · liquidity or covenant pressure into a downturn · recall or safety issue with verified material cost or production stop · loss of emissions compliance requiring material fines. **Moderate** — rising incentives two or more quarters · regulatory-credit income above 20% of operating income · tariff exposure on a large share of units without pricing offset · labor-contract cost step-up · EV losses widening faster than volumes. **Minor** — one-quarter production disruption · model-changeover timing.

**Pillars (weights):** cycle position & channel (days' supply, incentives, orders) 25% · balance sheet & industrial liquidity 25% · margins & unit economics 20% · growth & share 15% · valuation 15%.

## B. Auto suppliers

Earn content per vehicle on platforms awarded years in advance; volumes follow OEM production, margins depend on pricing pass-through (metals, energy, tariffs) and OEM bargaining power. Concentration and powertrain mix (ICE-dependent vs agnostic vs EV) drive long-run risk.

| Metric | Positive | Watch | Negative | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Growth over market** | Organic growth > production by 2+ pp | Roughly in line | Below production | Production = relevant regional light-vehicle build (Estimated, industry source) |
| **EBIT margin** | > 8% | 4–8% | < 4% | Tire makers and aftermarket can run higher — compare within sub-type |
| **Customer concentration** | No OEM > 15% | One OEM 15–25% | One OEM > 25% or top-2 > 40% | Check platform diversity within each OEM |
| **Booked business** | Grows, wins > roll-offs | Flat | Shrinking | Company-defined, nonbinding — Watch at most without delivered revenue |
| **Pass-through** | Contractual, lag < 2 quarters | Partial | Absorbed | Metals, energy, tariff recoveries |
| **Net debt/EBITDA** | < 1.5x | 1.5–2.5x | > 2.5x | Thin margins need low leverage |
| **FCF conversion** | > 60% of net income | 30–60% | < 30% | Launch capex and tooling distort single years |
| **Powertrain exposure** | Agnostic or EV-weighted with profitable programs | ICE-heavy with plan | ICE-heavy, no plan, or EV programs loss-making | Disclosure varies — often Estimated |

**What NOT to use:** booked business as revenue · growth without OEM production context · peer margins across tires, aftermarket and OE systems without adjustment.

**Red flags:** **Severe** — verified loss of a platform or customer above 10% of revenue · covenant or refinancing pressure · OEM financial distress affecting receivables. **Moderate** — customer concentration above band · unrecovered cost inflation two or more quarters · EV program cancellations with stranded capex. **Minor** — launch-cost quarter · strike or shutdown at one OEM.

**Pillars (weights):** cycle position & production exposure 25% · balance sheet 25% · margins & pass-through 20% · growth over market & concentration 15% · valuation 15%.

## C. Homebuilders

Land in, homes out: margins depend on land cost vs selling price net of incentives; demand depends on mortgage rates, affordability and existing-home supply. Asset-light land (options) lowers risk; owned land is where downturn losses come from.

| Metric | Positive | Watch | Negative | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Net new orders (YoY)** | > 5% | 0–5% | Negative | Units and value; check community count |
| **Cancellation rate** | < 12% | 12–18% | > 18% | Company definition (gross orders basis) stated |
| **Backlog** | Value growing | Flat | Shrinking | Signed contracts — convert at historical rates only |
| **Homebuilding gross margin** | > 24% | 19–24% | < 19% | State: ex-impairments? interest in COGS? |
| **Incentives (% of revenue)** | < 5% | 5–10% | > 10% | Mortgage-rate buydowns count as incentives |
| **Lots controlled via options** | > 60% | 40–60% | < 40% | Higher = less land risk |
| **Net homebuilding debt-to-capital** | < 20% | 20–35% | > 35% | Exclude mortgage-subsidiary warehouse debt |
| **ROE** | > 15% | 10–15% | < 10% | Pair with P/B for valuation |

**What NOT to use:** low P/E at peak margins · orders without cancellations and incentives · consolidated debt including mortgage-warehouse lines · closings guidance as achievement.

**Red flags:** **Severe** — cancellation spike above ~25% with falling prices or rising incentives (verified) · material land/inventory impairments with high owned land · debt maturities into a downturn. **Moderate** — incentives rising faster than ASP · orders negative two or more quarters · unsold completed (spec) homes rising · land spend rising while orders fall. **Minor** — weather or calendar shift in closings.

**Pillars (weights):** cycle position (orders, cancellations, backlog) 25% · balance sheet & land risk 25% · margins & incentives 20% · growth & returns 15% · valuation 15%.

## D. Household durables

Replacement and housing-turnover driven; appliances run thin margins at scale, furniture and branded electronics more. Promotions, raw materials, tariffs and retailer inventory decisions swing results.

| Metric | Positive | Watch | Negative | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Organic growth** | > 4%, volume positive | 0–4% or price-only | Negative | Split volume vs price/mix |
| **Gross margin vs own range** | Top half & stable | Mid-range or drifting | Bottom of range or falling | Indicative bands: appliances 15–25%, furniture 35–50%, branded electronics 35–60% |
| **Retailer channel inventory** | Lean, stable | Building | Destocking forcing cuts | Often commentary only → Medium confidence |
| **Housing-turnover sensitivity** | Low (replacement-led) | Moderate | High into falling turnover | Existing-home sales as context, Estimated |
| **Customer concentration** | No retailer > 15% | One 15–30% | One > 30% | Big-box concentration is common |
| **Net debt/EBITDA** | < 2x | 2–3x | > 3x | Lease-adjusted for own-retail models |
| **FCF conversion** | > 80% | 50–80% | < 50% | Through the cycle |

**What NOT to use:** cross-category margin comparisons (appliances vs furniture vs electronics) · price-led growth as demand strength · peak-housing P/E.

**Red flags:** **Severe** — retailer destocking plus inventory build plus margin collapse (verified) · covenant pressure · loss of a retailer above 15% of revenue. **Moderate** — price-only growth with falling volume two or more quarters · unrecovered tariff or raw-material cost · promotion intensity rising. **Minor** — one-quarter promotional calendar shift.

**Pillars (weights):** cycle position & channel 25% · balance sheet 25% · margins 20% · growth 15% · valuation 15%.

## E. Leisure products

Highly discretionary big-ticket and seasonal goods sold through independent dealers (RVs, boats, powersports) or mass retailers (toys, fitness). Dealer floor-plan costs and retail financing rates amplify the cycle; OEMs often sell more to dealers than consumers buy.

| Metric | Positive | Watch | Negative | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wholesale vs retail** | Retail ≥ wholesale | Wholesale ahead 1 quarter | Wholesale ahead 2+ quarters | Registrations/retail sales vs shipments |
| **Dealer / retailer inventory** | At or below pre-cycle norm | Above norm, falling | Above norm, rising | Units or weeks on hand, company or industry definition |
| **Revenue growth** | > 6% | 0–6% | Negative | Volume vs price split |
| **Gross margin vs own range** | Top half | Mid | Bottom or falling | Indicative: RVs 12–18%, powersports 20–30%, boats 25–35%, toys 45–55% |
| **Net debt/EBITDA** | < 2x | 2–3x | > 3x | Exclude captive retail-finance debt |
| **Seasonality & concentration** | Diversified | One season/property dominant | Single season or license > 30% of revenue | Toys: licensed-property concentration |

**What NOT to use:** shipment growth without retail data · peak-cycle P/E · comparing toy margins with RV margins.

**Red flags:** **Severe** — dealer inventory bloat forcing OEM-funded discounts and production cuts with falling revenue (verified) · captive-finance credit losses rising sharply · covenant pressure. **Moderate** — wholesale ahead of retail two or more quarters · dealer floor-plan stress · key license expiring without renewal. **Minor** — weather-driven season shift.

**Pillars (weights):** cycle position & dealer channel 25% · balance sheet 25% · margins 20% · growth 15% · valuation 15%.

## F. Apparel, footwear & luxury

Asset-light brand owners: products are designed in-house and mostly made by contract manufacturers; value comes from brand heat, pricing power and full-price sell-through. Demand runs on a brand cycle more than the macro cycle; the main warning signs are inventory outgrowing sales, rising markdowns, and a shift from DTC to off-price channels. Luxury houses add scarcity, price increases and tourist/Chinese-consumer exposure.

| Metric | Positive | Watch | Negative | How to read it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Constant-currency growth** | > 8% | 0–8% | Negative | Show reported alongside; multi-quarter trend |
| **Growth quality (channel)** | DTC full-price led, wholesale healthy | One channel carrying growth | Growth via off-price, discounting, or channel fill | DTC growth with falling gross margin is not quality |
| **Gross margin vs peers & own range** | Top half of peer set, stable/rising | Mid or drifting | Falling on markdowns | Indicative: mass athletic 40–48%, premium athletic 50–65%, luxury 65–75%+ |
| **Inventory-to-sales spread** | ≤ +5 pp | +5 to +15 pp, or > +15 pp one quarter | > +15 pp two+ quarters | See Inventory & channel rules (currency-basis test) |
| **Operating margin (IFRS/GAAP)** | > 15% | 8–15% | < 8% | Luxury higher; show adjusted vs reported gap |
| **Brand / franchise concentration** | No franchise > 30% of sales | One 30–50% | One > 50% | Often Not disclosed → state it |
| **Wholesale concentration** | No account > 10% | One 10–20% | One > 20% | 10-K / 20-F risk factors |
| **Cash conversion & leverage** | FCF ≥ 80% of net income; net cash or < 1x lease-adjusted | 1–2.5x | > 2.5x | DTC-heavy models carry large lease liabilities — include them |

**What NOT to use:** reported growth without constant-currency (or vice versa) · gross-margin comparison across luxury, premium and mass tiers, or across companies with different cost-of-sales policies (freight, duty, distribution) · "brand heat," awareness or social-media metrics as achievement · EV/Sales for mature brands · adjusted EBITDA without the SBC gap stated.

**Red flags:** **Severe** — inventory build plus verified markdown-driven gross margin decline plus falling revenue or cut guidance · verified liquidation into off-price channels damaging brand pricing · supply disruption from forced-labor detentions or sourcing-country sanctions with verified material impact · covenant pressure. **Moderate** — inventory-to-sales spread above +15 pp two or more consecutive quarters · wholesale order book declining · growth guidance cut while inventory rises · one franchise above 50% of sales · sourcing above 50% from one country with tariff exposure · rising marketing spend needed to hold growth. **Minor** — FX translation optics · one-quarter shipment timing · single-season weather effect.

**Pillars (weights — reweighted vs A–E):** brand demand & growth quality 25% · inventory & channel health 25% · margins & pricing power 20% · balance sheet & cash conversion 15% · valuation 15%.

## Mixed businesses & captive finance

Use segment analysis when several material models coexist: OEM + captive finance (Ford Credit, GM Financial, Harley-Davidson Financial Services) · EV maker + energy storage or software · luxury group with wines & spirits or selective retail · electronics group with games, music or financial services (route by dominant operating profit, often outside this packet). If segment data is insufficient, lower data confidence and say so.

**Captive finance:** rate it separately with bank-style metrics (credit losses, delinquencies, residual-value risk, leverage, funding mix — see the Banks packet's credit section); never fold its debt into industrial leverage and never count its earnings as automotive margin. A captive's credit or residual-value losses can still be a Severe flag for the group when verified and material.

## Peer-group rules

Do not compare: mass-market OEMs vs premium/luxury OEMs without adjustment · OEMs vs suppliers · suppliers across OE systems, tires and aftermarket without adjustment · homebuilders with land-heavy vs option-heavy models without stating it · appliances vs furniture vs electronics margins · RV makers vs toy makers · luxury houses vs athletic brands · DTC-led brands vs wholesale-led brands without channel-mix adjustment · USD reporters' reported growth vs non-USD reporters' reported growth without currency context.

## Valuation summary

| Branch | Preferred methods | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| A. Auto OEMs | Mid-cycle P/E, EV/EBITDA on industrial EV (ex captive), FCF yield; sum-of-the-parts with captive finance at P/B | Peak P/E; consolidated EV with captive debt |
| B. Auto suppliers | Mid-cycle EV/EBITDA and P/E, FCF yield | Booked business as revenue |
| C. Homebuilders | P/B and P/TBV vs ROE through the cycle; mid-cycle P/E | Peak P/E; P/B without ROE |
| D. Household durables | Mid-cycle P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF yield | Peak-housing multiples |
| E. Leisure products | Mid-cycle P/E and EV/EBITDA, FCF yield | Shipment-led growth multiples |
| F. Apparel, footwear & luxury | P/E vs growth, lease-adjusted EV/EBIT(DA), FCF yield, own-range multiple | EV/Sales for mature brands; peak-heat multiples |

Rules: state the assumed cycle position in every valuation rating; if undetermined, the valuation pillar caps at Watch. Mid-cycle means normalized margin on normalized revenue; label the result Estimated proxy unless every input is verified (then at best Calculated — verified inputs). Never rate valuation Positive on a trailing multiple computed from peak-range margins. Adjust EV for net cash and for lease liabilities consistently with the EBITDA used (IFRS 16 EBITDA excludes rent, so lease liabilities belong in EV). Homebuilders: justified P/B ≈ (ROE − g) ÷ (cost of equity − g), using through-cycle ROE. When the expectations module triggers (multiple above own 5-year range, or thesis resting on unproven segments such as robotaxi, humanoid robots, or a new sports category with no disclosed results), run it before rating valuation.

## Data availability notes

If a metric is not disclosed, do not invent it — use a proxy only when labeled **Estimated** (or **Estimated proxy**), and mark important missing metrics **Needs verification** or **Not disclosed**. Commonly undisclosed or inconsistent: dealer days' supply and incentives per unit (industry data, Estimated); supplier booked business definitions; homebuilder incentives (often commentary only); retailer channel inventory; leisure retail registrations (industry data); brand franchise concentration, full-price sell-through, markdown rates and wholesale order books.

## Default first-pass dashboard for Consumer Durables

Six cards: 1. Growth & cycle position (multi-year trend, stated cycle phase, branch) · 2. Margins & pricing (gross/operating margin vs own range and branch peers; incentives or markdowns) · 3. Inventory & channel (DIO, inventory-to-sales spread, dealer/retailer inventory, orders/cancellations) · 4. Balance sheet & cash (industrial net cash/debt, lease-adjusted where material, FCF conversion) · 5. Branch-specific card (A: regulatory credits & EV economics · B: customer concentration · C: land & options · D: retailer concentration · E: wholesale vs retail · F: DTC/wholesale mix & brand concentration) · 6. Valuation (mid-cycle multiple vs own history and peers).

Each card shows: value · Positive/Watch/Negative rating · provenance label (per the v4 standard) · one-line explanation. The first-pass score is always provisional unless: revenue, gross margin, inventory, and share count are verified from filings; cycle position is stated with evidence; and at least partial peer comparison is performed.

## Follow-up actions

Offer: 1. Verify cycle position (inventory, channel, orders) · 2. Deep dive: branch-specific weak point (A: incentives & days' supply / captive finance · B: customer and platform concentration · C: land, incentives & cancellations · D: retailer channel & promotions · E: dealer inventory & retail vs wholesale · F: inventory & wholesale channel / markdowns) · 3. Deep dive: tariffs, sourcing & currency · 4. Deep dive: valuation vs mid-cycle · 5. Compare vs branch peers · 6. Deep dive: quality of earnings (warranty, credits, impairments, SBC).

Priority logic: inventory-to-sales spread above +15 pp, or wholesale ahead of retail → recommend 1 then 2. Margins near multi-year highs with a low trailing P/E → recommend 1 then 4. Non-USD reporter or single-country sourcing → recommend 3.

## Score-change audit trail

Whenever a follow-up action changes the score, show all nine items: 1. previous score · 2. updated score · 3. metric that changed · 4. old status · 5. new verified value · 6. source document · 7. exact quote showing label and value · 8. pillar affected · 9. reason for the change.

Rules: if the exact quote cannot be provided, do not change the score — keep the metric at Needs verification. A score changes only when the newly verified metric changes a pillar's Positive/Watch/Negative rating; verification alone does not automatically change the score.

## Data confidence rules

**High:** recent 10-K / 10-Q / 20-F / 6-K / audited annual report with the exact table label and value quoted — covers revenue, margins, inventory, receivables, debt, leases, share count, customer-concentration disclosures. **Medium:** earnings release or investor presentation with a labeled table — often the only home of deliveries, orders, cancellations, incentives, channel mix and constant-currency growth. **Low:** aggregator-only data, industry-association volume data, channel checks, unlabeled numbers.

Rules: price and EV from aggregators may be used as Estimated. Industry volume data (SAAR, registrations, housing starts) is context, never verification. If a key metric is Low confidence, state how it affects the pillar score.

## Scoring template

| Pillar (A–E names vary by branch — see each section) | Weight A–E | Weight F |
|---|---|---|
| Cycle position & channel / Inventory & channel health | 25% | 25% |
| Balance sheet (& cash conversion in F) | 25% | 15% |
| Margins (& pricing power) | 20% | 20% |
| Growth (brand demand & growth quality in F) | 15% | 25% |
| Valuation (mid-cycle) | 15% | 15% |

Rate each pillar vs peers and own history: Positive = 2, Watch = 1, Negative = 0. Pillar contribution = pillar weight × rating ÷ 2; score = sum of contributions, 0–100%. ~75%+ strong on current evidence; 50–75% mixed; <50% with sufficient evidence and no verified Severe flag = weak on current evidence. Any verified Severe flag caps the score at 50% — always show the uncapped score, the cap applied, and the displayed score. A flag activates the cap only when its evidence is Verified or Calculated — verified inputs; a flag resting on user excerpts or relayed values stays a provisional warning. Two or more verified Severe flags usually mean high-risk / special situation. If cycle position is undetermined, the cycle and valuation pillars cap at Watch and the conclusion caps at "mixed, needs more evidence." The score is a research organizer, not a prediction engine.

## Conclusion template

End with exactly one of: **strong on current evidence** / **mixed, needs more evidence** (name the evidence) / **weak on current evidence** (score <50% with sufficient evidence and no verified Severe flag) / **high-risk, special situation** / **insufficient data** — plus data confidence (High/Medium/Low), the branch used, the stated cycle position, and a two-sentence thesis including what would break it. If cycle position is undetermined, the conclusion cannot exceed "mixed, needs more evidence." No buy/sell/hold language.

## Final sector checklist

1. Branch (A–F) identified by normalized operating profit; route-out list checked; captive finance separated.
2. Peer group built within the branch on model, price tier, channel mix and cyclicality.
3. Revenue, margins, inventory, receivables, debt, leases and share count verified with exact labels where possible.
4. Cycle position stated with evidence (macro for A–E, brand cycle for F), or declared undetermined with caps applied.
5. Inventory-to-sales spread computed with the currency-basis test; channel data labeled or marked Not disclosed; reported and constant-currency growth shown together for non-USD reporters.
6. Tariff, sourcing and customer concentration reviewed; quality-of-earnings and currency checks run.
7. "What NOT to use" respected; red flags graded with provenance labels before applying the Severe cap.
8. Valuation on a mid-cycle, lease- and net-cash-consistent basis vs own range and peers; expectations module run if triggered.
9. Score computed with branch weights; uncapped and capped scores shown when a cap applies; audit trail shown for any score change; conclusion states data confidence, branch, cycle position and unresolved gaps.
